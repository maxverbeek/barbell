Live mockups at true bar scale. Flip the system-state switches to see how each layout behaves when things are healthy versus when they need you — that's the decision that matters most, and it's hard to judge from a static picture.

J — What Apple would do newest

"Too dense and too starved at once" is a real diagnosis: things are packed tight and under-informative. That combination means the elements are too small and the spacing too tight — I'd been optimising for width all session, which is the wrong currency on a 1920px bar with an empty middle. Apple's answer would be roughly:

space is the material (~14px between items, never crowded) · words beat glyphs where a glyph needs a legend · no abstraction layer — the thing itself or nothing, never a dot standing in for it · state lives in shape, with colour as reinforcement rather than the message · nothing below ~11px.

How this resolved

The layout-shift problem never had a good answer because the question was wrong. A–G all assumed the bar's job is to display state, and then argued about how to compress it: collapse it (shifts), reserve it (leaves holes), dim it (F2), shrink it (G2). Every one of those is a way to fit something that shouldn't have been there.

Ask what each element changes and most of them don't survive. CPU at 80% doesn't tell you what to do — it tells you to open btop. So the bar wants the trigger, not the readout, and a permanent readout is rent paid for an occasional signal. Once resources move behind a menu, the pills lose their contents, and once the pills are empty they stop earning their container — which is exactly the thing you noticed.

H then over-applied it. Collapsing to one dot didn't just hide the readout, it destroyed the readout — three positions carrying three colours is information you can read without acting, and one dot is a button with a status light.

I then failed in a way worth naming: dense and starved at the same time. Packed glyphs that still need a legend. That pairing is the signature of optimising for width — which I did for eight sections straight, on a bar whose entire middle is empty.

J is the correction, and it's a different currency: space, not width. Nothing is abstracted — no dot stands in for a thing, so nothing needs a legend. The battery is a shape that empties. Items sit ~14px apart instead of 7px. It reads at a glance because there's air around each element, not because you learned what position 2 means.

J3 is what I'd build. J1's literal shapes and generous spacing, with hairline rules instead of pills — your point that a container only works when it's full is exactly right, and a rule separates without enclosing, so it can never look starved. Resources stay behind the menu (H's rule survives), and appear on the bar only when they cross a line worth interrupting for.

What actually survived the whole session: the exception rule (draw what needs you), no permanent readouts for numbers that only mean "go look", and no container without contents. The dots, the pills, the reserved slots and the crossfades were all machinery invented to fix a width problem that didn't exist.
