The mic, and where sound is going

Mic stateBar

Muted while something wants the mic is the state worth building for: you're talking and nobody can hear you. It's the only mic state that represents a mistake rather than a setting, so it gets the loudest treatment.

A live mic in green follows the existing rule — active and working, like the VPN. It also means the icon is absent whenever nothing is listening, which is most of the day.

Detectable natively: quickshell's Pipewire binding exposes link groups on the default source, so a non-empty list is "something is capturing" — no polling and no subprocesses. Verified on this machine with pw-record.

The speaker keeps one shape and changes colour. Blue means sound is going to bluetooth. Keeping the glyph constant matters because it's also the button into the audio menu — a target that changes shape is a target you have to look at. Bluetooth sinks report device.api = "bluez5", so it's a clean test.

Two things still undecided

1 · does the wifi name earn its width?

Your list runs from OnePlus 5 to Researchable: work from anywhere — 32 characters, roughly a fifth of the bar. Judge it against the longest one, not the shortest.

Truncating at 18 characters is the answer, and the list is why: four of your five names fit whole at that budget, so only the hotspot ever clips. It isn't a compromise applied to everything — it's a ceiling that one outlier hits.

It also keeps the part that disambiguates. Researchable: work… retains the colon, so it can't be mistaken for the office network — whereas abbreviating to the first word would render your boss's hotspot as Researchable, which reads as exactly the wrong thing. A shorter label that misleads is worse than a longer one that doesn't.

Where the islands can come back

Your own rule says where: a container works when it's genuinely full. They failed earlier because I wrapped things that come and go, so the capsule was left holding air. Wrap what's always present and always full and they work again.

Three things on this bar qualify — workspaces (always occupied), the always-drawn pair of speaker + battery, and the kube context (always a word). Everything conditional stays bare, so nothing can ever be an empty box.

K1 · islands on the constants, bare for exceptions

Exceptions sit outside the islands, unwrapped. That gives them a second signal beyond colour: anything not in a capsule is something that isn't normally there. The islands become the baseline and the bare icons become the news.

K2 · one island holding the whole right side

A single capsule around the status run. It's always full — the speaker and battery guarantee it — and exceptions join it rather than sitting apart. Closest to the look you had, and it does shift width as things appear, though only within the island.

K3 · islands as grouping, kube standing alone

Workspaces and status get capsules; the cluster name is left bare so it reads as a label rather than a control. Useful if you want production to shout — bare red text is louder than red text in a box.
